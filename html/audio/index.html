The <audio> Element

As defined in the HTML Living Standard, the <audio> element is used to embed sound content in a document. Like the <video> tag, it provides a native playback interface.

Accessible Implementation:

(Ensure a text transcript is provided below the player.)

<audio controls>
  <source src="podcast.ogg" type="audio/ogg">
  <source src="podcast.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
  Your browser does not support the audio element.
</audio>

Accessibility: The Necessity of Transcripts

Audio-only content is completely inaccessible to users who are deaf or hard of hearing.

WCAG Requirement: Alternatives for Time-Based Media

Success Criterion 1.2.1 (Level A): For prerecorded audio-only content, an alternative (such as a transcript) is provided.

A transcript should include not only the spoken words but also descriptions of meaningful sound effects or the tone of the speaker.

Understand SC 1.2.1: Audio-only (Prerecorded)

Usage Best Practices

  • The controls Attribute: Never omit the controls. Users must be able to pause, seek, and adjust volume to suit their needs.
  • Avoid Autoplay: Autoplay audio is highly disruptive and can interfere with screen readers. It is generally considered a poor UX and accessibility practice.
  • Preload Attribute: Use preload="none" or preload="metadata" to save bandwidth for users on limited data plans.

A11y Tip: Visual Indicators

If the audio is part of a larger interactive experience, provide a visual indicator (like a waveform or a "Now Playing" label) to help users identify which audio source is active.
